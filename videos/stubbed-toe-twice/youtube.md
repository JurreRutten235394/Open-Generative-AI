## Description
Stubbing your toe really does hurt twice, and it's not your imagination.

⚡ First comes the sharp OUCH. It rides fast nerve fibres (A-delta fibres) at roughly 5–30 metres per second, about as fast as a car.
🚶 Then comes the deep, throbbing ache. It rides slow nerve fibres (C fibres) at roughly 0.5–2 metres per second, about walking speed.
🦶 Your toe is one of the farthest parts of your body from your brain, so the slow signal shows up last: up to about a second later.
🧠 The fast pain tells you exactly where it hurts. The slow ache makes sure you don't forget it.

Sources: McGill University pain researcher Jeffrey Mogil, quoted by SELF: "The lag between first pain and second pain can be half a second or a second"; university neuroscience course material on nociception (fast A-delta fibres vs slow C fibres and their conduction speeds).
